This is the shape of what we bring back after 180 days: access to care first, then one row per category. For each, the AI's score on its own, its score with the agent, the difference, which is the uplift, and what each answer costs. There is a good chance the evidence adds little on clinical cases, and more on medical-dental integration, access and barriers to care. That is fine. The goal is to find where the evidence makes the most difference for the least cost, and put the effort there. Two more points. The overall score can rise because the AI models improve on their own or because the evidence adds more; we track both. And the dental test found that paying more for an AI model did not reliably buy a better answer, so cost belongs in the table. Limits: about 25 questions per round shows direction only, and the uplift can be small, zero or negative. Nothing on this slide has been measured yet.
