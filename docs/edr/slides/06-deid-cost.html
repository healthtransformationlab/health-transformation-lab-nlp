The third question comes first in practice: before anyone or any AI reads a real note, names, dates and record numbers have to come out. We planted 2,714 fake identifiers in 400 synthetic notes, and count how many each method leaves in, with its 95 percent range, and how many dental words it removes by mistake, words like Glickman or Gracey that look like names. There is no pass bar set as a leak rate yet. On synthetic notes a final check holds back any note with an identifier left, but only because we know every identifier we planted. On real notes nothing is known in advance, so what remains is a second scan for anything shaped like an identifier and a person checking one note in a thousand, about 3,000 dollars a million notes whichever method is used; that review is most of the cost. That is why the leak rate matters, and why a bar has to be set. Until a bar is set we use the top group: methods that could rank first on leaks. Are the less costly ones as good? No: patterns alone cost under a dollar but leave 645 in; Presidio with patterns about 5 dollars and 376; the Stanford tagger with patterns about 23 dollars and 226. The top group, the methods that could rank first, starts at about 98 dollars: the Stanford and OBI taggers together with patterns and the dental-word guard, 115 left in and 41 dental words removed; routing by identifier type is in the same group. Databricks' AI mask with patterns is in the same group and damages only 2 dental words, but at about 295 dollars. The paid services, Azure and Amazon, and our own LLM prompt have not been run, so we show their prices only, from about 18 to about 2,650 dollars.
