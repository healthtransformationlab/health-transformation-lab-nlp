This is the first of the three questions: what gum-disease status a chart shows, which researchers call phenotyping. Each row puts a method's cost for a million notes beside how often it gave the right status on our 223 synthetic review charts, with its 95 percent interval. The pass bar is the acceptance test: a method passes if it agrees with four gum specialists as often as they agree with each other. That test needs real specialists and has not been run, so nothing on this slide has passed yet. Until it is run, the stand-in is the top group: on resampled charts, could this method come first? Three can, and the table shows two of them: the whole-record rules and a small model trained on notes, ModernBERT-large with a rule for stage 4, at about 40 dollars a million notes on our own GPU, at an assumed speed and an illustrative price. The third, BioClinical ModernBERT-large with the same rule, is the same size and cost, and scores 90.1. Are the less costly models as good? Word counts cost under a dollar but are not in the top group: 85.7, and their interval reaches only 89.7. The open LLM we ran, Llama 3.3 70B, costs about 260 dollars and scores 83.9; the medical model MedGemma about 140 and 78.5. Neither is in the top group, so on this question paying more for a bigger general model did not buy a place in it. Frontier models such as Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Fable 5.1 have not been run on this question, so we do not know whether they are as good; we show their price only. Two cautions. The dagger rows use the same 2017 rules that made the answer key, which flatters them on synthetic charts. And synthetic notes are shorter and easier than real ones. If asked about the full table: it is in the appendix and in docs/results.md, every method with its interval and rank range.
