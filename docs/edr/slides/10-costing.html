How the cost figures were made, so a reader can check them. Hosted models are priced per token: each call sends the note plus the prompt, about 453 tokens, and gets about 20 back; at Databricks' list price for Llama 3.3 70B, checked October 26, that is about 260 dollars a million notes. The same tokens at Google's and Anthropic's list prices, checked October 28, give about 1,100 for Gemini 3.1 Pro and 5,500 for Claude Fable 5.1. If a frontier model reads the whole record with worked examples and reasoning, three calls a visit, with an assumed 20 thousand tokens in and 4 thousand out a call, it is about 260 thousand and 1.2 million; batch pricing halves either. Models on our own GPU are priced as hours at an assumed speed times an illustrative 4 dollars an hour, plus training: about 40 dollars for ModernBERT-large and 140 for MedGemma. CPU methods use the speed we measured, at an illustrative dollar an hour. Paid de-identification services are priced in their own billing units at list price. And the larger cost is people: reviewing a million visits by hand is about 33,333 specialist hours at two minutes a chart, while the acceptance test needs about 31, 233 charts read by 4 reviewers. If asked for a dollar figure for people: at an illustrative 100 dollars an hour, about 3,100 dollars to validate against 3.3 million to review everything.
