PerioBench is three tests, and people often blur them, so here they are side by side. First, phenotyping: what stage of gum disease does this visit show? That is the study's answer. Rules read the whole record; AI reads the note alone. We compare BERT-family models trained on our notes with large language models used as they come, and the best AI gets 203 of 223 synthetic charts right. Second, de-identification: before anyone or any AI reads a real note, names, dates and record numbers have to go. Here the AI does a different job, labelling each word private or not, and the best combination leaves 115 of 2,714 identifiers in, while keeping the dental terms that look like names. Third, tooth loss: was a missing tooth lost to gum disease? There is no text here, so no BERT: logistic regression and XGBoost read the probing measurements and extraction codes, with an AUC of about 0.9 on held-out teeth. Why it matters: five or more teeth lost to gum disease is what makes stage 4. Every number is synthetic, scored against the generator's own answer key. The drill-down for each test ranks its models the same way, with paired tests and what drives the result.
