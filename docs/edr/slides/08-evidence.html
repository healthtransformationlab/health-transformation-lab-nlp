Synthetic charts, scored against the generator's truth · 2017 AAP/EFP classification · rules and worked examples: docs/evidence_rules.md · 8

A dental chart holds seven kinds of evidence: the clinician's note; the exam form, with a gum score and a status checkbox; the procedure codes done and planned; the diagnosis codes on those procedures, done and planned; and the probing chart of gum measurements, turned into a stage with the 2017 American Academy of Periodontology and European Federation of Periodontology worksheet. Procedure codes are only a rough guess from the treatment, so they are compared, not used. When the other sources disagree, the rules take the first one, in a fixed order, that gives a status. The order matters a lot. Reading the gum measurements first, the first rule set, gets 234 of 240 synthetic charts right; it is the 234 on the results slide. The study design's order, exam form first, then diagnosis codes, the note, and the measurements last, gets about 173, 72 percent. On synthetic charts the measurements are the generator's own evidence, which flatters reading them first; on real charts the answer may differ. Four sources can be ranked 24 ways; each ranking is scored against the specialists, and the one that agrees best is kept. A radiograph reading can only raise the stage.
