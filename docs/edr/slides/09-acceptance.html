How do we know an answer is right? On real records there is no answer key, so the acceptance test is the bar: an AI method passes only if it agrees with the specialists at least as often as they agree with each other, the standard from the OHDSI 2023 plenary on case validation, with the LLM review work published in npj Digital Medicine in 2025. We also tested whether a general-purpose AI, a large language model, could stand in for the answer key. Reading each note itself, it matched the answer key on 83 percent of charts but only 53 percent of hard notes, the ones that leave out what the answer key is built from. Asked whether a method's answer was right, it caught most mistakes of weak methods, but only 26 percent of the best note model's, which are near-misses such as one stage off. So it cannot replace specialists yet. These are AI judgments on synthetic notes, checked against the generator's truth, made on the review notes before they were rebuilt for adults; the judge is being rerun on the new notes. If asked about statistics: kappa for agreement beyond chance, 95 percent bootstrap intervals over charts, and unsafe errors counted separately.
