Accuracy studies of chatbots on dental questions find real gaps. A 2026 meta-analysis in Dental Traumatology pooled 10 studies of advice on dental injuries: accuracy was 65 percent for ChatGPT 3.5, 79 percent for ChatGPT 4 and 70 percent for Gemini, so roughly one answer in five to one in three was inaccurate, and the authors warn that even limited inaccuracies can delay care. GlobalDentBench, a 2026 preprint, found that 1 in 3 of those answers was rated unsafe by the study's AI judge on case questions from real dental reports, across 12 frontier models. Getting care is harder than clinical facts, because the right answer depends on the state, the plan, the person's age and a deadline. Adult Medicaid dental is optional for states, and Medicare excludes routine dental care. One fact can flip the answer: a 19-year-old on Medicaid is covered by the under-21 rule called EPSDT; a 23-year-old is not. Nobody was measuring whether AI gets these right. That is the gap AccessBench fills.
