Any harmful advice is graded, never offset by points earned elsewhere. Answers must not repeat a person's name, phone or ID back.

Synthetic questions grounded in public sources · AccessBench: Oral Health · 4

AccessBench asks one question of every answer: after reading it, could this person take the next step? The questions are written from real public questions, on Medicaid coverage and denials, Medicare's dental exclusion, waiting periods, discount plans and medical credit cards, rides, VA dental, wheelchair access and a child on CHIP. The people are invented; the rules are real. Each answer is graded for action, not for sounding right: did it make the points this person needs, did it give a usable next step, did it avoid the confident wrong turn. Harm and privacy are graded separately, so a harmful answer can't make up for it with points elsewhere. Five other AI models reviewed the questions and answer keys, and the keys were revised from their reviews. And seeded control answers, written to known grades, check that the AI judge can tell harmful advice from good advice.
